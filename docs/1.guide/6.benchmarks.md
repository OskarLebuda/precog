# Benchmarks

> Eight synthetic sessions per arm, six navigations each, real Chrome against a production
> build, throttled to 250 ms of latency and 2000 kbit/s.

Reproduce with `pnpm bench`. The raw output lives in `bench/results/bench.json`.

## How to read this

::warning
The visitors are synthetic. Their click model weights links by position, so hit rate and
accuracy describe how the module does against **that model**, not against your users. The
timings are real: real Chrome, a real build, a throttled link.
::

**The median halves. The p95 does not move.** The tail is the navigations the model got wrong,
and no amount of prediction fixes those. If your problem is the tail, this is the wrong tool.

**It roughly triples wasted bytes**, from about 7 kB to about 19 kB per session. That is the
price of guessing three links instead of one.

**`off` is not "nothing".** The playground sets `takeOverNuxtLinkPrefetch`, so `NuxtLink` still
prefetches a link the cursor rests on in every arm. `off` means "Chrome and Nuxt on their own",
which is the baseline worth beating.

**`native` is the honest competitor.** Document rules at `eagerness: moderate` cost nothing and
need no model. In this benchmark they tie `off`, because a visitor who clicks soon after the
cursor lands does not give the browser enough hover to work with. That gap is what precog is
for. If your visitors hover longer, use `native` and save the money.

**The published table predates the first real call to Jev.** Its hit rate and accuracy came
from a heuristic standing in for the model, so treat them as a shape, not a result. `pnpm bench`
now requires a key and measures the real service; the table will be replaced once it has been
run that way.

**No cost column.** TypeSafe does not publish prices, so there is nothing honest to multiply
tokens by. Pass `BENCH_PRICE_IN` and `BENCH_PRICE_OUT` if you know yours.

## The visitor model

Each session is driven by a seeded generator, so all three arms face the same visitor:

- read the page in two to four scroll steps with pauses,
- pick a link with weight `1 / (rank + 1)` over the links in the list,
- move the cursor to it over a couple of waypoints,
- dwell 90 to 210 ms, then click.

The dwell is short on purpose. A long hover lets the browser's own prefetch-on-interaction
finish every time, and then no arm is measuring anything but the hover.
